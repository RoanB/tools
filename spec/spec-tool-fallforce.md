# Introduction

This specification defines the fallforce tool: a fall force calculator for single-rope-technique (SRT) scenarios, integrated into the tools application from the standalone `fallforce/index.html` prototype. It computes the peak force a drop of given height generates in a rope of given length and stiffness, so rigging decisions (absorption, friction, anchor choice) can be sanity-checked offline.

## 1. Purpose & Scope

**Purpose.** A single reference for the calculator's model, its explanations and its warning thresholds — the text and thresholds were reviewed against SRT practice when the tool was integrated (the prototype claimed fall factors above 0.3 are "atypical for SRT", which is wrong: falls onto a short cowstail routinely reach fall factor 0.5–1).

**In scope:** the `Fallforce` module (`/fallforce`), its template and client-side logic, the landing page card and the navbar item.

**Out of scope:** persistence (none — the tool reads and writes nothing), the finances area (own specs).

**Audience.** AI coding agents and the developer.

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Fall factor (FF) | Free-fall height `h` divided by the rope length `L` in the system (anchor → caver). Mass-independent. |
| Peak force | Maximum tension in the rope at the lowest point of the fall. |
| EA | Rope stiffness (modulus × cross-section), in newtons — the spring constant of the rope per unit length: `k = EA / L`. |

## 3. Requirements, Constraints & Guidelines

- **REQ-201**: The model is the linear-spring energy balance for a rigid anchor: `F = mg + √(m²g² + 2·EA·mg·h/L)`, elongation at peak `x = F·L/EA`. Fall factor is `h/L` and mass-independent.
- **REQ-202**: The rope options lead with their **EN type when one exists** ("Type A (EN 1891) — …", "Type B (EN 1891) — …") — the type is the principal argument of the dropdown; ropes outside EN 1891 lead with what they are instead. The dropdown is **ordered by type**: dynamic rope, type A, type B, cord, Dyneema. The list (rope ids, not EA values, because ropes share stiffness classes): Dynamic rope (EN 892) — Mammut Infinity 9.5 (EA 25 kN, ≈ 7% stretch at 80 kg — EA calibrated from its published impact force: 8.7 kN at FF 1.77; MBS 22 / knot 14 kN are conservative estimates — dynamic ropes publish no MBS); Type A — Beal Spelenium 10.5 (EA 42 kN, ≈ 3.5% at 150 kg, MBS 30 kN, knot 15 kN held 3 min with a figure-of-8 — per Beal's product page); Type B — Beal Spelenium 8.5 (EA 33 kN, ≈ 4.5% at 150 kg, MBS 22.6 kN, knot 12 kN — per Beal's datasheet); Type B — Petzl Push 9 mm (EA 40 kN, ≈ 3.7% at 150 kg, MBS 20 kN, knot 12 kN — per Petzl's datasheet); Cord (EN 564) — Beal Spelenium 8 mm (EA 23 kN, ≈ 6.5% at 150 kg, MBS 15.4 kN per Beal's catalog, knot ≈ 10 kN estimated — no published knot figure); Cord (EN 564) — Petzl Caving Line 7 mm (EA 33 kN, ≈ 4.5% stretch, MBS 14 kN, knot 8 kN — per Petzl's datasheet); Low-stretch Dyneema (EA 150 kN, ≈ 1% at 150 kg, MBS 30 / knot 19.5 kN — generic class estimates). The Petzl Caving Line is the default. EA is the **linear spring stiffness derived directly from the datasheet stretch** (EA ≈ 1.47 kN ÷ stretch — no artificial stiffening factor: the calibration closes against Beal's published EN 1891 test forces once the system's own absorption is accounted for, see REQ-209) and rides on each option as `data-ea`; the explanation modal shows the mapping.
- **REQ-203**: The page shows **no warning alerts except the two outcome boxes**: the rope-failure box (REQ-209) and the body-injury box (REQ-210). The fall-factor colour bar is the only indicator for fall factor, with rope-type-aware caution zones: dynamic rope green < 2, orange at 2 (the maximum possible — a fall factor 1 is a normal climbing fall on it); semi-static/static rope green < 0.9, orange 0.9–1, red ≥ 1. The rope kind is carried as an **explicit `data-dynamic` flag** on each option and must never be inferred from EA — the original `EA ≤ 50 kN` threshold broke when the semi-static stiffnesses were recalibrated into the same 23–42 kN range as the dynamic rope, leaving every bar green. **A fall factor above 2 is physically impossible** — the rope can never catch a fall longer than twice its own length (falling from the anchor with nothing above) — and a configuration that exceeds it is flagged in the summary line instead of showing forces. The prototype's "above 0.3 is atypical for SRT" claim is factually wrong and must not return. The ~6 kN human-body guideline (Petzl / rescue training) appears as explanation content and as the reference point inside the body-injury box — but forces between 6 and 10 kN fire no alert on their own, because ordinary FF 1 catches on semi-static rope land in that band.
- **REQ-204**: Technical explanation lives in a **modal behind a "How this works" button** next to the page heading; the page itself shows only inputs, result cards and one plain-language summary line. The modal contains: the fall-factor explanation with the colour zones, the datasheet-stretch → EA mapping, the per-rope FF 1 summary list, the worst-case caveat, the rule-of-thumb figures, what the two red boxes check — **what you feel** versus **what the rope holds** — and the **Sources** block (REQ-211).
- **REQ-208**: Under the result cards a one-sentence plain-language summary restates the outcome in body terms: the peak force in kN, how many times the caver's body weight that is, and the rope stretch in centimetres; at zero drop it says the rope simply holds the body weight; at a fall factor above 2 it flags the configuration as physically impossible instead of showing forces. Below the summary a **mini legend** (small secondary text, bold lead-in) compresses the modal content to the fall-factor colour zones — the datasheet-stretch → EA mapping, the rigid-worst-case caveat and the ~6 kN / knot-strength rule of thumb stay out of the page (modal only).
- **REQ-205**: The explanations state the model assumptions: rigid anchor, no belay-device absorption, no rope friction over deviations/rebelays — **and** reconcile the worst-case numbers with field experience: real systems absorb energy (knots, harness, body, friction), which is why falls below FF 1 on semi-static rope usually feel uneventful even when the model shows 7–10 kN. The 6 kN figure is explained as a risk guideline, not a physical cliff. The fall-factor explanation names the dynamic-rope exception explicitly (FF 1 = normal climbing fall).
- **REQ-207**: The explanation card explains what EA is: a stiffness written as a force — the load that would stretch the rope to double its length (idealized); read as "≈ 3 kN per 1% stretch" for EA ≈ 33 kN (a 4.5%-stretch rope), "≈ 15 kN per 1%" for EA ≈ 150 kN (the 1%-stretch Dyneema). The calculator converts the datasheet stretch figure linearly into EA and turns the rope into a spring with `k = EA / L`. Force grows with the **square root** of EA, and at FF 1 with an 80 kg caver the model yields ≈ 7.1 kN / 28% stretch (dynamic), ≈ 8.9 kN / 21% (Type A Spelenium 10.5), ≈ 8.0 kN / 24% (Spelenium 8.5), ≈ 8.7 kN / 22% (Petzl Push), ≈ 6.8 kN / 30% (Spelenium 8 mm cord), ≈ 8.0 kN / 24% (Caving Line cord) and ≈ 16.1 kN / 11% (Dyneema — approaching the breaking strengths involved).
- **REQ-206**: Every input must update the results live: sliders fire `input`, and the rope-type select — wrapped by select2 in this application — must also be listened to on `change` (select2 only fires change, not input), or switching rope type would leave stale numbers.
- **REQ-209**: Every rope carries its **breaking strength** (`mbs`, kN) and its **knot strength** (`knot`, kN) — datasheet values where available (Petzl Caving Line 7 mm: 14 / 8 kN; Beal Spelenium 8.5: 22.6 / 12 kN with a figure-of-8; Beal Spelenium 10.5: 30 / 15 kN; Petzl Push 9 mm: 20 / 12 kN), otherwise a conservative class estimate (dynamic rope 22 / 14 kN — dynamic ropes publish no MBS, their UIAA impact force is 8.7 kN; Dyneema 30 / 19.5 kN). All breaking strengths are **quasi-static pull-to-failure values** (EN 1891 §4.12.1 static strength; EN 564 for cords) — the same measurement method across brands, so the numbers are comparable; the manufacturers' "impact force" figures (e.g. Beal's 3.6–5.2 kN) are a different test (EN 1891 §4.10: 600 mm drop on a short sample, ≤ 6 kN) and must not be compared with the model's free-fall forces. The knot figure is a **certified hold, not a break number**: EN 1891 certifies the knotted rope holds the stated load for 3 minutes — a sustained guarantee, so a millisecond transient above it means "inspect and do not trust blindly", not "the rope has failed". The strength pair is shown under the rope select as "Breaking strength: X kN — Y kN with a knot (certified hold) — ≈ Z kN after system absorption", and a red error box fires when the model's peak force exceeds the **tolerated** threshold — the rope side of the page's two outcome boxes (body side: REQ-210) — using that wording and advising inspection plus a lower fall factor. The tolerance accounts for what the system absorbs beyond the rope: **E_sys ≈ 750 J** (knot tightening ≈ 250 J, harness and body ≈ 500 J), so the effective threshold is `√(knot² + 2·(EA/L)·E_sys)` — soft, short catches stay below it.
- **REQ-210**: A second red box warns about **the catch itself rather than the rope**: when the force on the caver's body still exceeds **10 kN** after the system's own absorption (the same **E_sys ≈ 750 J** as REQ-209) is taken out of the drop energy. That felt force is `mg + √(max(0, mg² + 2·(EA/L)·(mg·h − E_sys)))` — the model peak with the absorption credited to the system. The box reads that the rope may hold but "you will not be okay", and advises shortening the fall, putting more rope in the system, or using a more elastic rope. The trigger sits at 10 kN rather than the 6 kN guideline deliberately: ordinary FF 1 catches on nylon semi-static rope feel ≈ 7.7–8.5 kN (80 kg) and belong to accepted SRT practice, so a 6 kN trigger would fire constantly. With the 10 kN trigger, at 80 kg on 10 m the box fires from FF ≈ **0.45** on Dyneema, **1.37** on the Spelenium 10.5, **1.44** on the Push, and **1.72** on the Spelenium 8.5 / Caving Line; it never fires within FF ≤ 2 on the Spelenium 8 mm cord or the dynamic rope (max felt ≈ 9.5 kN). The stiff outliers are called out early, the soft ropes only at factors where a catch genuinely stops being survivable-unchallenged.
- **REQ-211**: The modal ends with a **Sources** block so every published figure is attributable. It lists the manufacturer datasheets the numbers were taken from, each a `target="_blank" rel="noopener"` link: Petzl Caving Line 7 mm (petzl.com), Petzl Push 9 mm (petzl.com), Beal Spelenium 8.5 and 10.5 (beal-planet.com product pages), the **Beal 2025 sport catalog** (beal.pl PDF — the whole Spelenium range: breaking loads, guaranteed ≥ 5 factor-1 falls, lab impact forces and elongations) and the **Mammut Infinity 9.5** manufacturer figures (climbing-gear.com archive, with a note that Mammut discontinued the Infinity and its construction lives on in the 9.5 Crag). It also names the **standards** (EN 1891 §4.10 impact-force test ≤ 6 kN and §4.12.1 static strength; EN 892 / UIAA 101 dynamic factor-1.77 drop; EN 564 cords) and the origin of the **~6 kN body guideline** (vertical-rescue / caving practice). A closing line flags explicitly **what is not manufacturer-published**: the dynamic-rope and Dyneema breaking/knot figures (conservative class estimates), the Spelenium 8 mm knot figure (~65% knot rule) and the 750 J system absorption (this calculator's own calibration, which closes against Beal's published EN 1891 test forces when the figure-of-8 is credited with ~250 J of it). Every figure quoted in the dropdown and the strength line must trace to one of these sources or be marked as an estimate.
- **REQ-212**: The tool is **public** — the module has no `secure()` method, so skeleton's dispatcher lets anonymous visitors through and `https://tools.buysse.io/fallforce` is shareable without login. This is safe because the page stores and reveals nothing about the visitor (no database, no persistence, client-side only). The base layout's **entire navbar renders only when a user is logged in** (`{% if user.email is not empty %}`), so the public page shows no app chrome — no brand, no area links, no user dropdown — just heading, calculator, modal and footer. All other areas keep their `secure()` login gates and keep redirecting anonymous visitors to `/login`.
- **GUD-201**: The tool is a plain module + template with client-side JS; no database, no persistence, no server state.

## 4. Interfaces & Data Contracts

- `App\Front\Module\Fallforce` (`app/front/module/Fallforce.php`) → `/fallforce`; assigns the rope-type options; template `fallforce.twig`.
- Inputs (client-side): body mass 40–200 kg (default 80), drop height 0–20 m (default 1), rope in system 1–100 m (default 10), rope type (EA preset).
- Outputs: fall factor (+ colour bar), peak force (kN + kgf), static equivalent (mg), dynamic rope stretch at peak (m + % of L).

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

- **AC-201**: Given the defaults (80 kg, 1 m drop, 10 m rope, Petzl Caving Line 7 mm), the page shows FF 0.10 and a peak force of ≈ 3.1 kN (≈ 4× body weight), with a green factor bar and no alerts.
- **AC-202**: Given a fall factor just below 0.9 on a nylon semi-static rope (Spelenium 8.5), the factor bar is green; from 0.9 it turns orange and from 1 it turns red — the bar's zones themselves fire no alert. Given FF 1 on the dynamic rope, the bar stays green — a fall factor 1 is a normal climbing fall for a dynamic rope, turning orange only at FF 2 (the maximum possible). The rope kind is read from the option's `data-dynamic` flag, never from EA (the EA ranges of rope kinds overlap since the linear recalibration).
- **AC-203**: The only warning alerts on the page are the two outcome boxes (rope failure, REQ-209; body injury, REQ-210); the 6 kN body guideline and the knot-strength explanation appear only inside the explanation modal.
- **AC-204**: The page is reachable from the landing page card and the navbar; both highlight it as an area of the application.
- **AC-205**: The modal reconciles the worst-case model with field experience (absorption via knots, harness, body, friction) so a model force of 7–10 kN is not presented as a guaranteed injury.
- **AC-206**: Given the default view, When the rope type is switched from Petzl Caving Line 7 mm (EA 33 kN) to the dynamic rope (EA 25 kN), Then the peak force drops from ≈ 3.1 kN to ≈ 2.8 kN without touching the sliders (the jQuery change binding picks up the select2-triggered change, EA read from the option's data-ea).
- **AC-207**: All extensive technical explanation renders only in the modal opened by the "How this works" button; the page itself shows inputs, result cards, the colour bar and the three-line mini legend under the summary.
- **AC-208**: Given the defaults, the summary line reads "peaks at about 3.1 kN — around 4× your body weight — … 94 cm of stretch" and updates with every input change; at zero drop it reads that the rope holds the body weight with no impact force.
- **AC-209**: Given a configuration with a fall factor above 2 (e.g. 20 m drop on 1 m of rope), the summary line flags the configuration as physically impossible instead of showing a catch.
- **AC-210**: Given the Petzl Caving Line 7 mm (knot strength 8 kN, tolerated ≈ 8.3 kN after system absorption), When the fall factor reaches ≈ 1.09 so the model peak crosses the tolerated threshold, Then the red box states the rope can fail and shows the absorbed threshold; on the dynamic rope (knot strength 14 kN, tolerated ≈ 14.1 kN) no configuration within FF ≤ 2 (peak ≤ 9.7 kN) triggers it.
- **AC-211**: Given Dyneema at FF 0.5 (80 kg, 10 m rope), the felt force ≈ 10.6 kN crosses the 10 kN body limit and the injury box fires — "the rope may hold; you will not be okay" — while the rope-failure box stays silent (11.7 kN peak vs ≈ 20.1 kN tolerated). Given the defaults or an FF 1 catch on the Spelenium 8.5, Push or Caving Line (felt 7.7–8.5 kN), neither box fires, and on the dynamic rope the injury box never fires within FF ≤ 2.
- **AC-212**: The modal's Sources block links every published figure to its origin — the Petzl Caving Line and Push pages, the Beal Spelenium product pages and 2025 catalog, the Mammut Infinity archive, and the EN 1891 / EN 892 / EN 564 standard names — and a closing note states which figures are estimates rather than manufacturer-published (dynamic/Dyneema strengths, the 8 mm knot figure, the 750 J absorption). All links open in a new tab with `rel="noopener"`.
- **AC-213**: Given an anonymous request (no session), `GET /fallforce` returns 200 with the full calculator (all seven rope options) and **no navbar** — no brand, no Finances/Fallforce links, no user dropdown, no logout — and no exception output. Given an anonymous `GET /finances` (or `/`), the visitor is redirected to `/login` as before. Given a logged-in request, `/fallforce` shows the calculator with the normal navbar and the Fallforce item marked active.

## 6. Rationale & Context

The standalone prototype (../fallforce) was correct in its physics but wrong in its safety framing: the FF > 0.3 warning treats standard SRT fall scenarios (cowstail falls) as anomalies, and the 6 kN alert mislabelled a body-load criterion as an anchor limit. The integrated version keeps the model and presets, and rewrites the framing.
